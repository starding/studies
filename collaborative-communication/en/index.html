Part 1 · A model of collaboration

Start with one person: under a goal, information drives decisions, and decisions drive results

  1. 1A decision depends on the goal and the informationThe goal defines what counts as good; information determines how well you can choose. Everyone picks what looks best to them, given their own goal and the information they have.
  2. 2Results are uncertainA good decision does not guarantee a good result every time, but over the long run the quality of decisions determines the quality of results.
  3. 3Results become new informationAfter one round the status has changed, and the next decision starts from the new status. The faster the loop turns, the faster you correct course.

Part 1 · A model of collaboration

Collaboration is many loops linked together: goals must align, information is dispersed, attention is limited

Communication: information flows between peopleTogether: the shared goal Writes the planInformationDecisionActionResult Builds itInformationDecisionActionResult Talks to usersInformationDecisionActionResult Each person holds only their own part; my decision needs information that you holdMy result changes your status; the parts add up to the overall result
  1. GoalThe result everyone wants together. Only with aligned goals do the parts decide in the same direction
  2. Information and decisionsInformation is dispersed across people; each decides on what they hold
  3. CommunicationThe channel through which information flows between people
  4. AttentionconstraintWriting and reading both cost attention, and everyone has only so much per day
  5. TruststateBuilt up over long collaboration; decides whether received information can be used directly

One proposition follows:Each decision is reasonable given that person's goal and information, yet the decisions may not fit together. For the overall result to approach the optimum, goals must align, information must arrive, and it should cost as little attention as possible.

Part 1 · A model of collaboration

Trust is what accumulates as expectations are verified by results; it decides whether information can be used directly

Expectationthe other person tells me what will happen next
DecisionI plan my own work on that expectation
Resultwhat actually happened when it came due
Comparedoes the result match the expectation?
Trusthow predictable the other person is to me
Carried into the next round: can I decide directly on the expectations this person gives me?

How trust updates each round

Delivered on time↑ rises
Things changed, expectation updated before the date→ holds
The date passes in silence: an expectation gap↓ falls

As in markets, the expectation gap often matters most. When things change, update the expectation before the date, much as a listed company revises its guidance ahead of results.

With higher trust, what changes elsewhere in the model

InformationThe expectations you receive can be used as a basis for decisions without re-checking
CommunicationThe same decision needs less communication, saving attention on both sides
Decisions and resultsEveryone’s loop turns faster, and the overall result is closer to the optimum

Part 1 · A model of collaboration

Four corollaries follow from the model; the texts and practices that come next all derive from them

Model: a decision depends on the goal and the information

1

Give all the information the other person needs to decide

Whether giving or asking, state the status, your judgment and the expectation. Whatever is missing, the other person has to fill in alone.

Model: attention is a constraint

2

Put the conclusion first and say it once

The reader can use it after one read, without opening links, digging through history or asking again. The more readers, the more the writing time is worth.

Model: information is dispersed, and collaboration has many rounds

3

People who were absent, and people who join later, get the same information when they decide.

4

State the date and scope, then deliver. If things change, update the expectation before the date.

Part 2 · Classic texts

Classic texts from open-source communities and internet companies

The internet industry’s earliest collaboration happened on mailing lists and forums. The person asking and the person answering did not know each other, lived in different time zones, and the answerer had no obligation to reply. Whether you got help depended on how clearly one message was written. Remote teams later wrote the same practices into their company handbooks.

Moving work forward: four texts map to four stages of one piece of work

GitLab Handbook

Write it down and keep it as public as possible, so people who missed the discussion can still follow.

Stage 2

Read and reply in your own time

Basecamp: How We Communicate

Write complete thoughts in full paragraphs; do not expect immediate replies; meetings are the last resort.

Stage 3

Decide and execute

Postmortems

Part 2 · Summary

The three premises these texts share are the model’s corollaries in other words

Premise 1 · Corollary 2

The reader’s time is worth more than the writer’s

Five more minutes of clear writing saves every reader one follow-up question.

Premise 2 · Corollary 3

Write it down where everyone can see it

People who were absent or join later can look it up. For a conclusion reached face to face, one written line is enough.

Premise 3 · Corollary 1

Every message moves one action forward

After reading, the reader knows two things: where things stand, and who does what next.

Corollary 4 (expectations and trust) gets little attention in these texts. We added it, drawing on how much finance cares about expectations.

Part 3 · Situations 1/2

Asking for information or action: first give everything the other person needs to decide

Asking

Ask with your own understanding

The product manager replies to a monitoring screenshot posted by Engineering

1
Check for yourself firstI looked at this chart and Monday night’s alert log.
2
State my understandingMy understanding is that the push queue backed up after 21:30, and that this is the same issue as the late price alerts.
3
Ask a question that is easy to answerDo you need me to assess how many users were affected, or is knowing the cause enough?

Requesting work

Who, the deliverable, the date, and where it goes

The product manager asks Engineering for the current rules

1
Name the person@Engineering, please
2
State the deliverable, date and placepost the trigger rules for price alerts and the daily push cap in this thread by Thursday 18:00.
3
Say what it is forI will put them in section 2 of the proposal, to judge whether the cap needs to change once the queue is split.

Discussing a proposal

State the problem first, then the proposal

The product manager opens a discussion in the project channel

1
Frame the problem as user experience or business outcomeProblem: for price alerts in the first 20 minutes after the open, the price has already moved by the time users receive them.
2
List proposals point by pointI have three proposals: 1. … 2. … 3. …
3
Give reasons separately for the contested pointProposal 2 touches on switching to SMS. Engineering and I think we should not: it has no advantage in cost or delivery time.

Part 3 · Situations 2/2

Giving information: lead with the conclusion and the expectation, then the basis

Progress updates

Update when there is progress, and say what is still open

The product manager replies to Operations; the release time is still being confirmed

1
Conclusion in the first sentence@Operations The cause is identified and a draft proposal is ready: [link].
2
Status, cause and fix, one sentence eachOn Monday, alerts for about 3,200 users arrived 18 minutes late on average, because they share a queue with news pushes. The fix is to move them to a separate queue.
3
Give an expectation even before it is finalThe release time is still being confirmed. I will update here by 18:00 today.

Sharing a document

Three lines before the link

The product manager posts the proposal in a newly created project channel

1
What problem this document solvesThis channel is for fixing the price-alert delay.
2
The core conclusionShort term: a separate queue, targeting Thursday. Long term: prioritised push tiers, see section 3. [link]
3
Who should read which part, by when@Engineering, please read section 2 by Wednesday 12:00 and confirm whether Thursday is feasible.

Replying to a request

State your understanding and a completion time

The manager says: when you reply to Operations, give the cause and the expected fix date

1
Restate my understandingGot it. I understand Operations needs two things from us: the cause of the delay, and the fix with its release date.
2
Give a completion timeI will add both in the Operations thread by 17:00 today.
3
Write face-to-face conclusions back to the threadFor the record, the conclusion from my conversation with Engineering just now: a separate queue, live Thursday.
